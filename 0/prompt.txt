> **Fact check compares words, not meaning**
> The reviewer found that a model could attach a real quote to an invented sentence. The fix now compares each fact sentence with its quote, in code, with no model involved.
> * **What it catches:** numbers, names and place words that aren't in the quote, and sentences that share fewer than half their key words with it. "I flew combat missions" is dropped.
> * **What it can't catch:** wording that reuses the quote's words but changes the meaning. "I never taught piano for forty years" shares 4 of 5 key words with "taught piano for forty years", so it would pass.
> * **Why it matters:** the app promises that every displayed fact is backed by a source, and this check backs that promise only partly.
> * **Worth knowing:** the quote-in-source check and the number and name rule still apply, so the gap is mostly negation and reordering.
> If that's too loose, a stricter option is to show the quote itself next to each fact instead of the model's wording.
